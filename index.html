How the fares work

The fare is for one block. It moves the same way an airline fare does, from three numbers. A block also has to fit in a single open stretch between 9 AM and 4 PM, so a day can be open for 30 minutes and closed for 2 hours.

  1. How far out you book. Six weeks ahead is the cheapest. Inside a week, it climbs. Same-day is the walk-up fare, or the seat may already be gone.
  2. How full that day is for this block. Four billable hours can be sold. Hours that cannot be packed into a block of the length you picked raise the fare. If none fit, the day is unavailable.
  3. How full that week is. Demand is the share of that week’s hours already taken on the November 11 snapshot, leaving out weekends and holidays. It does not change when you switch block length.

Worked example, so the arithmetic can be checked. A 30-minute block, base $187.50, five days out (lead 1.30), with 1 of 4 hours not packable (load 1.12), in a week under 25% held (Quiet, 0.90): $187.50 × 1.30 × 1.12 × 0.90 = $245.70, which rounds to $250.

Closed on this calendar: Thanksgiving (Nov 26–27), Christmas Eve and Christmas (Dec 24–25), New Year’s Eve and New Year’s Day (Dec 31–Jan 1), MLK Day (Jan 18), and Presidents’ Day (Feb 15).
