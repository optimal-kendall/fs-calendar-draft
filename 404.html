How the fares work

The rare thing being sold is Kendall’s calendar, not a new product for every request. Each offering on the menu takes a set number of hours. The fare for those hours moves the same way an airline fare does, from three numbers:

  1. How far out you book. Six weeks ahead is the cheapest. Inside a week, it climbs. Same-day is the walk-up fare, or the seat may already be gone.
  2. How full that day is. Each weekday has four hours for sale. The last open hour on a day carries a last-seat premium.
  3. How much demand that week is seeing. A quiet week stays closer to the advance fare. A week that’s filling fast moves up, even if you book it early.
The algorithm, in full
fare = round_to_$25( base × lead × load × demand ) floor: 45% of base ceiling: 3× base Lead time (days out) 42+ 0.55 28–41 0.65 14–27 0.80 7–13 1.00 ← base sits here 3–6 1.30 1–2 1.65 same 2.00 Load (hours already held that day, of 4) 0/4 1.00 1/4 1.12 2/4 1.28 3/4 1.55 last-hour premium 4/4 sold out Demand (that week’s pace) Quiet 0.90 Normal 1.00 Busy 1.20 Hot 1.40 Base fares (Reid defaults) Working session $375 · 1 hour Alumni hour $250 · 1 hour (Intensive graduates) Team session $750 · 2 hours AI Ramp Day $1,500 · 4 hours (full day’s capacity)

Example: a working session, base $375. Six weeks out, on a quiet empty day: $375 × 0.55 × 1.00 × 0.90 = $186, rounds to $175. Two days out, last hour on a busy day: $375 × 1.65 × 1.55 × 1.20 = $1,151, capped at 3× to $1,125.

The calendar in this draft is a live demo of that formula. Held seats and weekly demand are simulated so you can see the range. In production, held seats would be real bookings, and demand would be the actual pace of inquiries against remaining hours that week. Closed days in this draft: Thanksgiving (Nov 26–27), Christmas (Dec 24–25), New Year’s Day (Jan 1).
